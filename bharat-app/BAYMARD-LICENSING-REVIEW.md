## RISK ASSESSMENT

### If images are screenshots FROM Baymard's articles/reports:

**HIGH RISK.** Baymard's terms explicitly prohibit reproduction, redistribution, and public display of their content. Even "limited citations" require prior written approval from Baymard. Using their proprietary research imagery in a public portfolio without permission would violate Section 3.4.

### If images are the author's own mockups illustrating Baymard principles:

**LOW RISK.** If the author created original screenshots/mockups to demonstrate how Baymard's publicly documented principles apply to the Sorted cart, there is no copyright issue. Referencing publicly known UX research findings (e.g., "show full order cost", "use large thumbnails") is not restricted — these are general UX knowledge. The restriction is on reproducing Baymard's proprietary materials, not on discussing their published conclusions.

### If images are annotated screenshots of public e-commerce sites:

**MEDIUM RISK.** If the screenshots are of public websites (Amazon, etc.) with Baymard-style annotations, the risk depends on whether the annotations reproduce Baymard's specific visual style, framework, or copyrighted analytical overlays.

## RECOMMENDED ACTIONS

### Option A: Replace with original mockups (SAFEST)

Create original screenshots or annotated mockups that illustrate the same cart/checkout principles applied to Sorted's own UI. This eliminates all licensing risk while preserving the design argument.

### Option B: Contact Baymard for permission

Per Section 3.4: "Limited citations of the content may be granted upon request by contacting us for approval." Request written permission for portfolio use. Baymard may approve citation in a non-commercial design portfolio context, but this is not guaranteed.

### Option C: Replace images with text descriptions

Remove the visual tab panels and replace with a text-based list of the 9 principles with descriptions. No imagery = no copyright risk. The design argument remains intact through prose.

### Option D: Keep and accept risk

If the images are publicly available screenshots (not from behind Baymard's paywall), the practical risk of enforcement against a personal portfolio is low. However, this is a legal judgment, not a design one.
