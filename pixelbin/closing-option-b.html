Reflection

What I Cut
and Why

V1 had 14 flows. Every idea made it to the canvas — AI Influencer, Script to Video, Edit Video, Motion Control — all as separate journeys. 170 frames across the Figma file. It felt comprehensive. It was also unusable as a product spec. No engineering team could ship 14 parallel workflows for a POC.

The hardest cut wasn't a flow — it was the interaction model. Early versions had Pixie respond only when asked. The AI was capable but passive. Flipping it to proactive — where Pixie analyses an uploaded image and proposes edits before the user types anything — was a bet that took three rounds of stakeholder review to land. The concern was that users would feel the AI was overstepping. The evidence said otherwise: removing the blank-prompt problem reduced the gap between upload and first action to a single tap.

What's still open: batch mode at true catalogue scale (1,000+ images), AI influencer consistency across 50+ generations without drift, and whether the structured interview pattern holds when users want to skip questions and just generate. These are the edges I'd push next.

Design Principle

The best AI doesn't wait for instructions.
It proposes a plan and asks if you agree.
